Two hidden dials

Beyond what towns regulate, the corpus scores how. Two findings stand out.

Dense ≠ strict

How densely a town writes its laws is statistically independent of how much it controls conduct (correlation ≈ 0). Florida writes the wordiest codes in the country — yet is among the least controlling. Verbose and permissive is a real combination.

A town’s quirks date its code

Rules travel in bundles. Billiard halls + circuses + pawn shops mark a 1920s-era code. Short-term rentals + solar + e-scooters mark a recent rewrite. What a town regulates hints at when it last opened the codebook.

How to read this. Built from the open LOCUS-v1 corpus of U.S. local ordinances. “Regulates X” means a town has a substantive law whose title mentions X (titles are far cleaner than OCR’d bodies, but this undercounts). Topics and scores are machine estimates; text is OCR’d. Plain-language versions are faithful paraphrases for understanding — not legal advice, and not the law. Always check the actual text, shown beneath each one.
